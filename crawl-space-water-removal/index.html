Crawl Space Water Removal: What Reno Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Water That Is Not Clean

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Termites and Other Pests

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

Day one

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

The answer turns on where the water came from. Policies often respond to abrupt accidents, for example a water line breaking under the home. Groundwater, surface flooding and slow leaks that went on for months usually are not under a standard policy. Check your policy or call your agent, and we can document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your claim.
A shop vac can handle a small puddle of clean water. It will not dry joists and subfloor, and a box fan mostly moves damp air around. Crawl spaces are also tight, dark and may have live wiring or sewage in the water. Anything beyond a shallow puddle is better pumped out and dried by a crew with the right gear.
It is best to stay out as long as there is standing water. Electrical cables and boxes are common down there, and wet ones can energize the water. The water may also contain sewage or soil bacteria, and the space is cramped with poor air. A look through the access door with a flashlight is enough before you call for an inspection.
The fix follows from the source of the water. A plumbing leak needs a repair, while rain and groundwater problems usually call for better drainage outside. Helpful steps include clearing gutters, extending downspouts and grading the yard so it falls away from the walls. You will get our findings at the end, along with practical ways to stop it happening again.
Removing the standing water is quick, generally a matter of a few hours. Expect 3 to 5 days after that for the lumber, with dehumidification and air movement going the whole time. Thick beams, a dirt floor with no liner, or water that sat for weeks can add time. The job ends when the wood tests dry, whatever the date says.
