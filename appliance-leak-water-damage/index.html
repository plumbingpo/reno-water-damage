Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Reno

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

Our Reno crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
  • Washer supply hoses made of rubber that get weak over the years and burst from the pressure.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Hidden Mold at the Back

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Cabinet Damage

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Flooring and Subfloor

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Wall Bases and Trim

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Which appliance leaked and whether it was a fast flood or a slow seep.
  • How many rooms the water reached, including any ceiling below an upstairs laundry.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.
