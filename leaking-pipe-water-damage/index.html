Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Reno

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. For Reno homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Check your policy and ask your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • What the floors and finishes are made of, because certain materials cannot be dried in place.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
  • The number of rooms and floors affected, such as a ceiling underneath the leaking line.
  • Any mold found, since it must be contained and removed before the drying can be completed.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
  • Years of excessive water pressure straining valves and joints until one of them starts to weep.
  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
  • A P-trap or drain connection under a sink that loosens and drips each time the basin empties.
  • A crimp ring or solder joint that gives out inside a ceiling or wall cavity where nobody can see it.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Rotted Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Damaged Flooring

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Our Leaking Pipe Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

2

Make Targeted Openings

We make targeted openings to reach wet cavities, remove drywall, insulation or cabinet parts that cannot be saved, and bag any moldy material so spores are not spread through the house.

3

Dry the Structure

Drying equipment stays in place, typically for 3 to 5 days, until moisture in the framing and subfloor is back to normal. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Final Treatment and Records

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and you receive photos along with the moisture logs. That paperwork proves the area was dry ahead of repairs and can support a claim.
